## What this demonstrates

| Design claim | Where |
|---|---|
| Emotion handled before information | Turn 1 — real numbers, not "don't worry" |
| **Every number computed or looked up, never generated** | Rank, loan ceiling, EMI, fees (D7) |
| We show options we don't monetise — and admit our gaps | The 6-option map; the UPTAC refusal |
| **The money conversation gates the college list** | Stage 4 before Stage 5 |
| **The bot talks him out of the expensive option his father named** | Newton paragraph (D1) |
| Every recommendation carries a caution | All three picks |
| He's better off even if he never converts | The "Ask This" 5 |
| **Connect mentor is from the college he actually chose** | `?college=niat-adypu` (D5) |
| Unlisted colleges → honest answer + demand signal | The VIT variant (D5) |
| Cost discipline | 11 outbound, batched questions, tap-buttons |

## What would make it wrong

- Reaching the college list before the loan math
- A recommendation without a caution
- "Don't worry, everything will be fine"
- **Any number without a source and a date — or a JoSAA/UPTAC cutoff we guessed at**
- A generic /connect link instead of one filtered to his college
- Pushing Connect more than once
- Never being willing to say "none of these fit you"
