00 / CONTEXT

Executive overview

The system is designed to improve the quality of a high-stakes education decision, not to maximise admissions.

Every year, roughly 14.5–16 lakh students register for JEE Main while the JoSAA seat pool across IITs, NITs, IIITs and GFTIs is about 67,323. The majority therefore enter a compressed, confusing private-admission market where tuition can reach ₹8–28 lakh and much of the advice is commercially motivated.

Campus Critique is a WhatsApp advisory agent for that moment. It behaves like a careful senior: it first stabilises the student, understands academic and financial context, widens the option set, stress-tests affordability and downside risk, and only then narrows to options. It can recommend against spending, explicitly disclose uncertainty, and end with a useful audit checklist even when no admission follows.

Thesis / locked decision D1The bot's success metric is never admissions. There are no per-admission referral fees, no commercial field in the match engine, and a non-zero advised_against_spending_rate is a health signal—not a failure.
~95%JEE candidates outside JoSAA seat pool

Large audience, short decision window, limited trustworthy guidance.

7advisory stages

From emotional stabilisation to a consent-based handoff.

5guardrail rails

Safety, state, grounding, numeric and commercial-integrity controls.

0allowed ungrounded numbers

Fees, rank, EMI and cutoffs must come from computation or verified data.

01 / PRD

Product requirements

The user problem, product behaviour, requirements, measures and safety commitments.

1.1 The real user problem

The surface question is often “which college should I choose?” The underlying state is more complex: panic after a disappointing percentile, conflicting advice, a parent being asked to risk retirement savings, and uncertainty about whether an institution's degree, placements and refund policy are what its marketing implies.

Aarav · panic-stricken aspirant

18, Lucknow, 78.4 percentile, believes “IIT or nothing”. Needs stabilisation, a wider map and financial clarity.

Ramesh · risk-bearing parent

May sacrifice savings or property. Needs plain loan, collateral, degree and downside facts.

Priya · conflicted dropper

Improved once but is exhausted. Needs an honest second-drop analysis and alternatives.

1.2 Product objective

Turn a panicking, badly-informed 17–18-year-old into a calmer decision-maker who understands the option map, can explain the financial downside to their family, and knows what to verify before paying.

The product earns trust through disinterest: it is allowed to say “none of these fit you”. A recommendation is an output of the process, not the product's purpose.

1.3 The seven-stage advisory model

Stabilise

Recognise emotion specifically; provide proportionate context; intercept self-harm or severe distress before continuing.

Locate

Collect percentile, board result, geography, budget, family income, intent and risk appetite using short WhatsApp prompts/buttons.

Widen

Show JoSAA rounds, state quota, established private colleges, new-age institutions, drop year and BCA/BSc plus self-learning.

Stress-test

Run loan capacity, EMI/downside and degree-route checks. This is the mandatory gate before college names.

Narrow

Rank suitable options from verified facts only; every institution carries a cautionary counterweight.

Equip

Deliver an “Ask This” pack: degree, paid internship, all-in cost, refund policy and independent-student questions.

Hand off

Offer a college-specific independent senior connection or an honest “not covered yet” message; follow up only with consent.

1.6 Ethics and privacy

Minor-safety invariantThe system does not ask for Aadhaar, banking credentials or application passwords. Phone numbers, income, scores and distress flags live in a dedicated wa schema/project with its own retention and purge path. A crisis message stops recommendations and CTAs immediately.

Consent is explicit for follow-ups; “STOP” is honoured. The system uses Tele-MANAS 14416 / 1800-891-4416 as the crisis route and does not present itself as a mental-health professional.

Technical requirements

A deterministic pipeline with a model in the middle—not an agent with authority over facts or safety.

The target stack is Next.js API routes on Node.js 22 / Vercel, TypeScript, direct Meta WhatsApp Cloud API, Upstash QStash, Supabase PostgreSQL 16 with pgvector, Promptfoo in CI, and self-hosted Langfuse over OpenTelemetry. Decision D9 keeps this bot operationally separate: its own repository, Vercel project, Supabase project and WABA, connected to Campus Critique by a nightly ETL over public APIs.

2.2 Data is selected by question shape

LayerUseMechanismModel authority
A · ComputePercentile→rank, loan ceiling, EMI, total costPure arithmetic in TypeScriptNone
B · Structured lookupFees, degree vehicle, placement facts, exam dates, later JoSAA tablesSQL fact blocks with source and updated_atNone
C · Prose retrievalVerified reviews and campus-life texturepgvector RAG over approved textMay phrase retrieved evidence only

RAG is not used for ordered cutoff tables: vector similarity does not understand numeric ordering. The output prompt receives a fact block, and a post-generation scanner rejects any ₹, LPA, percentage or rank token not present in the block.

2.3 Five-rail guardrail engine

Rail 1 · input safety

Decomposed Hinglish distress classifier. Crisis signals pre-empt every other route.

Rail 2 · dialog state

Finite-state stage machine; the Stage 4 gate prevents premature recommendations.

Rail 3 · grounding

Retrieved facts, source, date and institution caution are attached before generation.

Rail 4 · output scanner

Numeric token and required-caution scan; failure falls back to a template.

Rail 5 · integrity

Blocks commercial persuasion, unapproved claims, fake-persona language and unsafe CTAs.

2.4 Deterministic formulations

indicative_rank = (100 - percentile) × candidates_appeared ÷ 100
max_loan ≈ 4 × annual_family_income
monthly_emi = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n - 1)
four_year_cost = tuition + hostel + mess + deposits + known mandatory fees

These are approximate decision-support calculations, not loan sanction or official rank declarations. Each answer carries the applicable caveat. Example: 78.4 percentile and 14.5 lakh candidates gives roughly 3.13 lakh rank; it does not promise a specific college.

2.5 Request lifecycle

  1. Validate Meta webhook signature and persist an idempotency key.
  2. Queue the event; merge rapid successive messages within four seconds.
  3. Load the session and current stage from wa_sessions.
  4. Run distress and scope rails before any model call.
  5. Compute/query the fact block and update stage state transactionally.
  6. Generate bounded text through the model adapter, or use a deterministic template.
  7. Scan numbers, cautions and banned commercial patterns; log the decision and send only on pass.
  8. Emit structured telemetry with stage, grounding result, latency, token usage and failure reason.

2.7 Release gates

CI must fail closedPromptfoo suites cover crisis language, prompt injection, unlisted colleges, missing cautions, hallucinated numbers, premature college names, commercial pressure and replayed messages. Required invariants: distress recall 100%, ungrounded numeric claims 0, banned commercial phrases 0, deterministic match output byte-identical for the same fact/profile inputs.

2.8 Cost and observability envelope

Under the documented India rate assumptions, an 11-outbound-message golden-path conversation is approximately ₹1.27 in messaging fees plus model and compute costs, or roughly ₹2.60 all-in in the working estimate. At 10,000 students/month the planning estimate is about ₹28,850/month. These figures are estimates tied to the chosen Meta pricing assumptions and must be revalidated before launch.

03 / VALIDATION

Conversation and test scenarios

The transcript is a behavioural specification: every response demonstrates a requirement, a rail, or an architectural decision.

Golden-path profile · Aarav18, Lucknow · 84% in Class 12 · 78.4 percentile · father income ₹6 lakh/year · considering an expensive new-age institution after JoSAA disappointment. The bot must not jump to a college list.
TURN 01 · STABILISERail 1 / Stage 1
Aarav
78 percentile aaya. IIT/NIT to possible nahi hai. Life khatam lag rahi hai.
Bot
78 percentile after a full year of prep genuinely stings—main pretend nahi karunga ki it doesn't. But you're not the exception: most JEE candidates do not get a JoSAA seat, and there are still real routes. Pehle ek quick check: are you safe right now, or are you thinking of hurting yourself?

The reply validates without false reassurance, adds context and performs a safety check before information.

TURN 02–03 · LOCATEStage 2 / batched intake
Aarav
Safe hu. Lucknow, boards 84%. Papa around 6 lakh kamaate hain. Budget ka idea nahi, loan lena padega.
Bot
Samajh gaya. Main tumhare liye 4 cheezein map karunga: rank, family affordability, options outside IIT/NIT, and what to verify before paying. Is loan ko comfortable EMI ke hisaab se dekhna hai ya maximum possible budget ke hisaab se?

Questions are batched to reduce message cost and cognitive load; income is a decision input, not a lead score.

TURN 04 · WIDENDecision D2 / Stage 3
Bot
Tumhare map mein six branches hain: JoSAA/CSAB spot rounds, home-state options, established private exams, new-age colleges, another attempt, and BCA/BSc CS plus self-learning. Main sirf apni eight colleges ko “best” nahi bolunga—pehle map dekhna zaroori hai.

The bot shows options it does not monetise, and discloses that state cutoffs are not verified in v0.

TURN 05–07 · STRESS-TESTDecision D7 / mandatory gate
Bot
Roughly, banks may assess borrowing around 4× annual family income, so ₹6L income suggests a ceiling near ₹24L—not a promise. Loans above ₹7.5L often need collateral. If a ₹18L course is financed over seven years, the EMI can be around ₹28–30k/month depending on rate. If placement takes six months, who carries that EMI? Also: do you want GATE, government work or an MS later? The degree-granting university matters for those routes.

The system computes the values and phrases a fact block; the model never invents them. College names remain gated until the three dimensions are complete.

TURN 08 · NARROWStage 5 / caution invariant
Bot
Based on the budget and risk you described, I would not put Newton at ₹26.9L first. NIAT is more financially plausible to investigate, but verify the reported average placement number and ask whether it is for the full batch. A cheaper BCA/BSc route with a strong portfolio may be safer if the family cannot absorb the downside.

A recommendation is paired with a caution; “advised against spending” is a valid, measurable outcome.

TURN 09 · EQUIPStage 6 / audit pack
Bot
Before paying any deposit, ask: (1) Which university is on the final degree certificate? (2) How many students got paid internships and what was the median stipend? (3) Is the quoted fee all-in? (4) What is the refund policy after semester one? (5) Can you speak to a current student they did not select for you?

The student leaves with a useful artefact even if they never convert.

TURN 10–11 · HAND OFFDecision D5 / consent
Bot
If you choose NIAT, I can connect you to an independent NIAT senior for ₹99/30 minutes; they are paid by us, not NIAT. If you prefer, talk to your dad first. I can check in in about three weeks—reply STOP anytime.

Handoff is deep-linked to the chosen college, offered once, and followed by explicit opt-out language.

Delivery, governance and open risks

The plan makes the deterministic core independently testable before model sophistication is added.

4.2 Locked decisions that shape the design

D1

No per-admission revenue. Against-spending is a KPI.

D2

Recommendations may include options outside the eight tracked colleges.

D5

Handoff follows the college the student actually chooses; unknown colleges create demand signals.

D7

JEE data uses deterministic compute and SQL, not RAG.

D8

Use a swappable Decision port; shadow-evaluate Jev later, never depend on an unproven model.

D9

Standalone project and isolated data boundary, bridged by nightly ETL.

D10

No message cap; engaged students are the users most worth serving.

D12

Realistic nine-week build with three engineers, not a one-week promise.

4.3 Risks and mitigations

RiskMitigationResidual honesty
Facts become staleSource and updated_at on fact blocks; nightly ETL; output dates facts where useful.Some college claims remain early-signal data, not mature placement statistics.
Model hallucinates a numberCompute/SQL boundary, fact block, numeric scanner and deterministic fallback.Release gates reduce risk; they do not replace source maintenance.
Safety language is missedDecomposed Hinglish classifier, red-team suite, pre-emption and supervisor alert.Human review and monitoring remain necessary during pilot.
Advice feels like salesNo commercial relationship field, banned persuasion patterns, against-spending dashboard tile.Independence is a governance property that must be audited continuously.
Data compliance changesIsolated project, consent records, no national identifiers, cycle-based purge policy.Legal review is required before public launch, especially for minors.

4.4 Faculty review checklist

  • Can the team demonstrate the Stage 4 gate with a low-income profile?
  • Can the system show where every number in a reply came from?
  • Can a reviewer inspect an unknown-college log and its source query?
  • Can a distress message be replayed and shown to stop the funnel?
  • Can the same profile/fact snapshot produce the same match output twice?
  • Can the team explain which parts are implemented, which are specified, and which depend on external credentials or data refresh?
